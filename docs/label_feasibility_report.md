# Are major-outage labels trustworthy? Label feasibility for the six-state onset rows

Steps 3 and 4 of the project plan. Tables are generated by `pipeline/11_audit_major_labels.py` into `reports/label_audit/` (file names in brackets). Rows are the 2014-2023 training opportunities from stage 10: one non-overlapping 24-hour county window per NOAA-recorded storm onset. Validation (2024) counts are reported as usable-sample sizes only. For 2025, only opportunity counts and structural county-year coverage were computed; no 2025 outcome window was summarized.

## Bottom line

1. **Positives are plentiful; trustworthy negatives are not.** At 5,000 customers out, 7.8% of training windows are major and 9.8% are established non-major under the strict rule. The other 82% are unknown.
2. **Unknowns are unknown because EAGLE-I omits rows.** 93% of them (25,581 of 27,618) would become non-major if absent runs were read as zeros. EAGLE-I's documentation says absence means "zero or a gap in data collection," so that reading is not allowed.
3. **Known labels are strongly selected toward large counties.** At 5,000 customers, labels are unknown for 93% of windows in counties under 25,000 people and 35% in counties over 500,000. Unknown shares also fall over time (87% in 2014-2017, 77% in 2022-2023) and differ by hazard (49% tropical, 94% winter).
4. **A fully observed all-zero window almost never occurs** (3 in training, 2 in validation). Any-outage occurrence is not learnable; a thresholded major/non-major target is, on a selected population.
5. **Customer denominators fail validation in three states.** The modeled county customers (MCC) cover 34% of North Carolina's customers. EAGLE-I's own 2024 totals exceed EIA state counts by 28% (FL), 34% (NC) and 106% (SC). Percentages can be computed only for counties with a validated MCC (60% of training windows).
6. **Some majors are data artifacts.** 103 counties record a peak above their valid customer count or population; 22% of 5,000-customer majors fall in those counties, and 9% rest on a single 15-minute run.
7. **Absent runs during big outages are often collection gaps.** In training windows reaching 1,000 customers, runs missing between two observations of at least 1,000 average 0.56 per window for tropical and 0.70 for synoptic-wind windows, against 0.07 for convective wind [collection_gaps.csv]. Counts that size do not fall to zero and recover within 15 minutes.

## 1. Label states

- **major**: an observed value in `[t0, t0 + 24 h)` is at or above the threshold. A crossing establishes a positive even in a sparse window.
- **non-major**: every observed value is below the threshold and coverage holds. `strict` = all 96 scheduled runs observed; `cov90` = at least 87 observed and no gap longer than 4 runs (1 hour).
- **unknown**: everything else. Null values and absent runs are not observations.

The rule is asymmetric on purpose: one observed crossing proves a positive, but absence never proves a negative. The price is selection bias (sections 3 and 4).

## 2. Reporting coverage [coverage_state_year.csv, coverage_dec31.csv]

| Source years | Convention observed in the six states |
|---|---|
| 2014-2016 | Zero-outage rows omitted; no null values |
| 2017-2021 | Zero rows omitted; null values in 0.1-12% of rows |
| 2022-2024 | Zero rows appear for every county but only intermittently (0.2-22% of rows); no nulls |
| 2025 (structural only) | Null values in 1-30% of rows; zero rows only in four NC and four TN counties. Another convention change, relevant to the final test |

- Median county observed share of state collection runs rose from 4-27% (2014, by state) to 16-49% (2024). Florida is best observed, Tennessee least.
- Runs with no row anywhere in a state: 500-1,150 per state in 2015 (Alabama 16,482, when only about 30 of its counties appeared), under 100 from 2019 except South Carolina (737 in 2020, 673 in 2022). With zeros omitted, these mix collection failures with periods when tracked utilities had no outages.
- Dec 31: 2014-2021 files end at 00:00 UTC; 2022-2025 contain full Dec 31 data, which cleaning v3 had discarded. The Southeast pipeline keeps it.
- Five Tennessee counties (Benton, Decatur, Hardin, Humphreys, Perry) never appear in EAGLE-I; their opportunities are excluded.

Non-major labels come from counties that always have a few customers out, so that every run produces a row. Large counties qualify; small rural counties almost never do. A model trained on known labels would therefore learn mostly from large counties, and its evaluation would describe that population, not all storms.

By hazard (strict unknown share): tropical 49%, coastal surge 62%, synoptic wind 72%, lightning 77%, rain/flood 82%, convective wind 83%, hail 86%, winter 94%. By state: Florida 67% unknown, Tennessee 88%.

## 5. Pre-existing outages [t0_state.csv]

The t0 state uses the last EAGLE-I run that started at least 15 minutes before `t0`.

- That run has no row for 55% of training windows, so the t0 state is unknown for most rows.
- At 5,000 customers, 183 windows were already major at `t0` (177 stayed major: *continuing*), 1,911 majors crossed after being below at `t0` (*new*), and 535 majors had an unknown t0 state.
- New and continuing majors answer different questions; the primary target should be one of them, with the other reported separately (decision O-5).

## 6. Severity completeness [severity_completeness.csv]

- Only 17% of training windows with any positive observation (4,837 of 28,256) observe all 96 runs. That complete-window cohort is what the preliminary severity baseline used.
- Of the 2,623 majors at 5,000 customers, 60% have a fully observed peak; for the rest the observed maximum is a lower bound. 231 contain interior gaps bracketed by values of at least 1,000 customers, which probably hide part of the peak.
- A severity model conditional on major windows would mix exact peaks and lower bounds unless restricted or modeled as censored (decision O-7).

## 7. Candidate definitions of major (training only) [threshold_candidates.csv, threshold_by_size.csv]

| Threshold | Major windows | New / continuing | Counties with a major (of 529) | Counties with fewer customers than the threshold | Tropical share of majors | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 10,232 | 6,080 / 1,050 | 494 | 3 | 9% | Routine for large counties; 77% of windows in counties over 500k are major |
| 2,500 | 5,323 | 3,611 / 389 | 438 | 11 | 13% | Broad geography; still frequent in big counties |
| 5,000 | 2,623 | 1,911 / 177 | 366 | 49 | 19% | Uncommon (8%) with 452 validation positives |
| 10,000 | 1,068 | 806 / 78 | 247 | 130 | 29% | Rare (3%); 228 validation positives; most counties under 50k people cannot cross it |
| 25,000 | 325 | 252 / 32 | 122 | 279 | 39% | Too few validation positives (89) for stable comparisons |

Trade-offs:

- **A fixed count measures absolute burden.** It suits crew and mutual-aid staging, but small counties rarely or never qualify. In counties under 25,000 people, the 15 windows crossing 10,000 have a median peak of 1.15 times the county's modeled customers, so they are probably artifacts.
- **Percentages measure local severity** but need a valid denominator. With MCC validated and peaks plausible, 5% of customers gives 3,081 training majors; 40% of training windows (all of North Carolina, plus counties with implausible peaks) have no valid denominator. The plan keeps percentages as a supporting analysis, reported separately, never mixed into the count label.
- **Robustness at 5,000 customers** [label_robustness.csv]: 232 majors (9%) rest on one 15-minute run, 574 (22%) are in counties with implausible peaks, and 270 (10%) start from a zone event covering only part of the county.

## 8. Options for checkpoint B

| Choice | Options | What the evidence says |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | 2,500 / 5,000 / 10,000 customers | 5,000 keeps majors uncommon while leaving about 450 validation positives; 10,000 halves that and excludes most small counties by construction. Choose by the intended action, not by model scores. |
| Non-major rule | strict / cov90 | cov90 gives 66% more negatives with gaps of at most an hour; both remain concentrated in large counties. |
| Pre-existing outages | new crossings primary / all majors | New crossings match "anticipate a new major outage"; continuing majors are a different, easier question. |
| Implausible counties | keep / exclude / report separately | 103 counties (22% of 5,000-customer majors) have peaks above their customers. |
| Population statement | results apply to covered windows only | Required whatever is chosen: label coverage, not storms, defines who is in the evaluation. |

Not recommended: reading absent runs as zero (forbidden, and contradicted by collection gaps during large events); any-outage occurrence targets (3 complete zero windows); mixing count and percentage labels.
